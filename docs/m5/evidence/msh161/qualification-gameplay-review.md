# MSH-161 (Competitive Gameplay Diagnosis): independent qualification gameplay review

Reviewed 2026-11-11. Scope: four completed unaided-start games, both players' complete move histories, fixed Stockfish 500,000-node unrestricted and played-move screens. No previous engineering reports or suspected mechanisms were consulted; no engines were run and no production files were changed. This is an independent gameplay interpretation of qualification evidence, not the final study diagnosis.

## Evidence and limits

Sources are `qualification/qualification-oxidation060-start/{report.json.gz,analysis-summary.json,analysis/*.json}` and the corresponding `qualification-goldfish113-start` files. Game indices and plies below are **zero-based**, matching receipt names `gNNN-pNNN-...`. Candidate is engine 0. Each played decision has both query receipts: 301 decisions against Oxidation and 339 against Goldfish, 1,280 total queries. Only 180 and 148 comparisons respectively meet the summaries' usable-comparison checks. Completed queries do not mean confirmed assessments. Many flagged values use an earlier exact score after a later bound update, and some unrestricted/played searches disagree even on the same first move. Such cases remain uncertain.

Expected-score values below are the reference's mover-relative WDL expectation, not measured winning probabilities or the candidate's own evaluation. Even usable 500k screens require deeper same-root confirmation before consequential labels. In particular, the endgame p95 Goldfish comparison rates the *same* move Re4 differently between unrestricted and restricted queries; this is uncertainty, not evidence that playing Re4 was an error.

Candidate won both Oxidation games (177 and 124 plies), won Goldfish as White (107 plies), and drew as Black (232 plies, native fifty-move claim). These are real outcomes, but four unaided games neither establish a stronger opponent nor estimate relative strength reliably. Oxidation's and Goldfish's resistance must be judged over diverse qualification positions before fixing the study reference.

## Complete-game interpretation

**Oxidation game 0, candidate White.** The opening and early middlegame are competitive but loose on both sides. Repeated reference opportunities arise around ...Be6 p7, ...g6 p15 and ...f6 p31; the candidate also appears to miss some stronger replies (Nf3 p8, g5 p18, Nd3 p32), mostly uncertain screens. Oxidation's p35 ...Rae8 has a usable drop from 0.3545 to 0.0815 for Black, but this does not imply an uninterrupted White conversion. White's e4 p42 appears to relinquish much of an advantage compared with h4 (0.9975 versus 0.5195, uncertain). The later usable screens identify Qh2 p52 (0.9995 versus 0.7895, alternative Ng3) and c4 p54 (0.9505 versus 0.5460, alternative Nb7). Both played continuations permit ...e5 counterplay. Oxidation repeatedly fails to exploit these openings; ...h6 p55 offers another chance. White eventually consolidates a large advantage, coordinates its rooks and queen, and wins the long ending. Qxg4+ p122 exchanges queens into a winning minor-piece/pawn ending according to both screens, followed by passed-pawn promotion and mate. This is useful positive conversion evidence, despite earlier missed opportunities. The long finishing phase is not itself evidence of a defect: the reference gives saturated winning WDL throughout much of it.

**Oxidation game 1, candidate Black.** Early development is approximately competitive; ...Bb4 p5 has a modest usable screen loss (0.4055 to 0.3475), insufficient alone for a serious opening indictment. The game remains contestable into move 21. Oxidation's c4 p40 appears decisive: the screen goes from near equality to near loss, and Black immediately finds ...Nh5 p41, then ...Qf6 p43. The PV illustrates king-side threats and coordinated queen/knight play, not simply a material pickup. Black sustains the advantage through active rook play, queen invasion, an exposed opposing king, and the final ...Nc2# p123. Although the pivotal p40/p41 screen is uncertain, the actual sustained conversion is clear. This game supplies positive attacking and conversion examples and a reference tactical-resistance concern.

**Goldfish game 0, candidate White.** Both sides remain near reference equality through opening exchanges and the middlegame; the candidate handles a queen-versus-two-rooks material imbalance with an active bishop and central pawns. Goldfish's ...Qb5 p51 is the clearest usable opponent screen error: Black expectation falls from 0.5000 with ...Rc8 to 0.0110. White responds with Ba3 p52, d5 p54, d6 p56 and Rc7+ p58, matching or closely following the reference's strong continuation. White combines the passed pawn, active rooks, and bishop effectively, later wins material, promotes, and mates with Rf1# p106. This is a particularly valuable successful opportunity-response sequence to retain alongside failures. It also shows why simply counting candidate errors without opponent errors and responses would misdescribe the contest.

**Goldfish game 1, candidate Black.** This is the richest diagnostic game. At p11, ...O-O has a usable screen drop from 0.4855 (...d5) to 0.0055; the reference continuation begins g4 and attacks the castled king. Goldfish instead plays e4 p12 and apparently misses this opportunity. At p15, ...Nfd7 again appears to allow h4 and a strong king-side attack, but Goldfish chooses c4 p16; these latter comparisons are uncertain. The game returns to roughly balanced play. Later, Goldfish's f4 p46 offers a possible advantage through ...Qc6; Black's ...Nf5 p47 and ...a5 p49 do not appear to preserve all of it. Goldfish's Bd4 p52 offers a second opportunity via ...Rxb1, but Black's ...Ng6 p53 screens near equality. The strongest tactical-conversion question is Bxe5 p66, ...Re8 p67, Bxc4 p68, ...Qxe5 p69: Black finds the strong rook move but then apparently gives up a substantial advantage by exchanging queens instead of ...Bxc4. The alternatives preserve pressure along the e-file and win material in the reference continuations. All these tactical opportunity comparisons remain uncertain pending confirmation. The actual game simplifies into a rook ending. Both sides sustain practical resistance and eventually draw; later screen fluctuations do not establish a missed forced win. This was neither a smooth defensive draw nor evidence that Goldfish consistently punishes the candidate's weaknesses.

## Candidate gameplay questions, not mechanisms

1. **King safety and timing of central counterplay:** Goldfish g1 p11/p15 and the opponent's missed responses p12/p16. Determine whether a concrete attack exists and whether ...d5 or ...Ne4 prevents it. Do not infer an evaluation or search defect from move labels alone.
2. **Preserving advantage against counterplay:** Oxidation g0 p42/p52/p54. Determine whether the candidate unnecessarily frees ...e5 or loses useful piece activity. Several related opportunities occur in one game; that is not independent frequency evidence.
3. **Recognizing and completing tactical opportunities:** Goldfish g1 p46–53 and p66–69. The latter is especially informative because the candidate first finds the strong ...Re8, then apparently chooses a premature simplification. A good first tactical move is not proof of correct continuation judgment.
4. **Opening effectiveness:** Candidate opening decisions sometimes offer early attacking chances, but small losses elsewhere and the two successful White games argue against a blanket opening diagnosis. Diverse master-supported starts plus separate unaided games are needed to distinguish opening selection from general gameplay resistance.

These questions coexist with demonstrated strengths: coordinated attacks against Oxidation, exploiting Goldfish's queen-placement error, passed-pawn support, and successful long conversions. No code mechanism, priority for implementation, or strength claim follows from these four games.

Unrestricted discovery is optional because initial alternatives exist. Inspect the completed pilot once; keep unstable consequential claims unresolved or request a narrow follow-up instead of automatically expanding all budgets.

This selection covers both players, early deterioration, opponent response, missed opportunity, and strong conversion controls. It deliberately excludes a claim that the rook ending was a missed win and excludes forcing a production hypothesis. The coordinator plans four diverse master-supported qualification roots, paired against both references (16 additional games, eight per reference). This is a useful practical next qualification step alongside the already completed unaided starts, with equal practical thinking time. Select a fixed suitable stronger reference from actual competitive resistance and useful error punishment across that diversity; the present 2–0 and 1.5–0.5 scores do not establish that either opponent satisfies the study's stronger-reference requirement.
