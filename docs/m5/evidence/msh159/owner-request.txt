# mShogi: Investigate and Improve Classical Chess Evaluation

MSH-158 (Chess Decision Corrections) fixed a real search problem, but two important decision failures remain.

I now want to examine whether the classical evaluation function can correctly assess positions where the important consequences are known.

**The objective is better Chess decisions, not closer numerical agreement with Stockfish.**

## 1. Investigate the unresolved failures

Start with these development positions.

**0UOyM: pawn race and king route**

mShogi prefers Kg6 over exf3+. Independent analysis strongly favors exf3+ because it leads to a better king route and pawn race.

**0m5mI: bishop blockade**

mShogi initially prefers Bd6 over Kc3. At longer thinking times, it finds Kc3 but still appears to misunderstand the blockade and king penetration.

Also use 28pJK as a useful control. MSH-158 corrected its move selection, but its reported evaluation remains substantially different from the independent reference.

These are diagnostic examples. Do not tune the engine to recognize these exact positions.

## 2. Separate search failures from evaluation failures

For each important position, establish the consequences as reliably as possible.

Use tablebases or proven continuations where available. Otherwise use independently reviewed reference analysis with the necessary uncertainty.

Examine selected intermediate positions along the important continuations.

Determine:

- What is the actual position?
- What does mShogi's static evaluation report?
- Which evaluation terms produce that result?
- Does the evaluation correctly rank important alternatives?
- Does the error come from material, king activity, pawn structure, passed pawns, mobility, or another general feature?
- Does the search reach the relevant position and use its evaluation correctly?

Keep the same position, side to move, history, and evaluation perspective when making comparisons.

Do not treat Stockfish centipawn values as calibration targets. Its evaluation scale and methods differ from ours.

A difference in score is not enough to establish a defect. We need evidence that the evaluation ranks relevant positions incorrectly or causes poor decisions.

## 3. Examine the existing classical evaluator

Review the current evaluation design as a complete system.

Identify the terms that assess king activity, pawn races, passed pawns, blockades, piece coordination, and conversion.

Check whether these terms work together as intended.

Consider established conventional Chess evaluation techniques. Use reference implementations where they help explain a general solution.

Do not assume that an existing heuristic works well merely because it has tests or is based on a familiar Chess principle.

Focus on the mechanisms supported by the observed failures. Do not perform unrelated optimization or a general architectural rewrite.

## 4. Test general improvements

You are authorized to implement and test experimental classical evaluation changes.

You do not need to prove a correction will work before testing it. A clear technical hypothesis and a controlled experiment are sufficient.

For each candidate change:

1. State the problem and expected effect.
2. Implement a general correction.
3. Test the motivating positions and other relevant development positions.
4. Measure actual move decisions at five seconds.
5. Examine regressions and wider effects.
6. Retain the correction only if the evidence supports it.

Use selected fifteen-second tests when they help separate evaluation from search depth.

A better static score is not sufficient. The correction must improve meaningful decisions or provide a clearly established evaluation benefit that supports those decisions.

Do not add position-specific bonuses. Do not modify pruning or search behavior merely to force a preferred move.

If the investigation establishes that the principal problem is still search coverage rather than evaluation, report that finding. Do not disguise a search problem as an evaluation problem.

## 5. Use the existing benchmark

Reuse the 240-position benchmark from MSH-156.

Use development positions for diagnosis and experiments. Include independent successful controls and positions with similar mechanisms.

Keep the 80 held-out positions sealed until the candidate and comparison procedure are frozen.

Use five seconds as the primary comparison. Retain the existing one-second checks.

Avoid running the entire benchmark after every experimental change. Use focused development tests first.

When a candidate is ready, compare it with the frozen predecessor on the full benchmark. Reuse verified baseline results where possible.

Report supported improvements, regressions, unchanged decisions, and unresolved judgments separately.

Preserve exact mate and tablebase results.

## 6. Strict scope

Do not substitute any of the following for the assigned objective:

- General search-speed optimization.
- History or draw-claim optimization.
- New benchmark construction.
- Additional general architecture assessments.
- Opening-book research.
- Learned evaluation.
- Shogi or Xiangqi work.
- A competitive tournament.
- A broad parameter sweep without a specific hypothesis.

Record unrelated findings briefly, but do not implement them.

Keep MSH-153 (Classical Compensation Calibration) on hold unless the evidence establishes a relevant general valuation problem. Do not activate it solely because it is next in the backlog.

## 7. Work limits and engineering process

Use a single focused engineering task with a descriptive handle.

Follow AGENTS.md and the risk-based verification policy. Preserve authoritative native rules, history, outcomes, restoration, and cancellation.

Use independent review for the diagnosis and retained corrections.

You may implement and integrate supported evaluation changes without requesting approval for each experiment.

Keep new active engine computation within 2,400 seconds. Do not run opponent matches or substantial new studies without approval.

If a reasonable set of distinct experiments fails to identify an effective correction, stop. Explain the technical obstacle and recommend the next general mechanism to investigate.

Do not replace a failed evaluation investigation with unrelated engineering work.
