# mShogi: Next Conventional Chess Engineering Round

I approve the general direction of the revised roadmap following MSH-154 (Chess Adversarial Assessment) and MSH-155 (Chess Time Scaling).

I want one additional improvement to our development process before substantial engine changes: establish a broad, independent decision-quality benchmark using existing public Chess databases.

Our objective is to build a demonstrably strong conventional Chess engine. We must measure the quality of its decisions, not merely its search speed, depth, or agreement with individual reference moves.

## 1. Establish an independent Chess decision benchmark

Research and use suitable free public resources, including:

- Lichess Puzzle Database for tactical problems of different types and difficulty levels.
- Lichess Position Evaluations for diverse positions with strong reference analysis.
- Strategic Test Suite (STS) for positional and strategic decisions.
- Syzygy tablebases for exact endgame results.
- Other established Chess-engine test collections where useful.

Verify licenses, provenance, data formats, and limitations before use.

Select a diverse, manageable collection rather than downloading entire databases. Aim initially for a few hundred positions across tactical, defensive, positional, strategic, and endgame situations, adjusting the size when justified.

Include both straightforward and difficult problems.

Preserve source identifiers, position state, available move history, and independently established expectations. Do not invent missing history or assume that a FEN contains complete repetition information.

Distinguish exact mating or tablebase results from published preferences and finite reference-engine opinions. Allow multiple acceptable moves where justified.

Use the existing experiment infrastructure. Do not create a new testing framework unless a specific missing capability requires it.

## 2. Separate development tests from independent validation

Maintain two distinct collections.

Development positions may guide investigation, implementation, and tuning.

Held-out positions must not influence those decisions. Use them to determine whether improvements also work on unfamiliar positions.

When a held-out position becomes part of development, move it into the development collection and replace it with independent material.

Prevent closely related positions from the same game or problem family from leaking across the split.

Freeze test expectations before evaluating a candidate. Do not change expected answers merely to make the engine pass.

The existing eight-position benchmark becomes a regression collection. It must not serve as the principal evidence of general improvement.

Keep routine checks efficient. Larger benchmark runs belong at coherent engineering checkpoints, not after every minor change.

## 3. Use practical thinking times

MSH-155 established that some important decisions improve when mShogi receives five seconds instead of one.

For conventional-engine development:

- Use five seconds as the primary decision-quality comparison.
- Retain one-second tests for fast-play performance.
- Use fifteen seconds, or longer focused searches, when needed to distinguish search limitations from evaluation problems.

Compare candidates at equal thinking time. Record completed search depth, actual work, elapsed time, selected moves, and relevant continuations.

Do not assume deeper search or greater node throughput proves better play.

For eventual competitive qualification, prefer realistic game clocks with increments and suitable independently rated opponents. That requires separate approval.

## 4. Continue the two complementary engineering workstreams

**Workstream A: search efficiency**

Investigate and correct demonstrated computational waste in native move queries, checked transitions, attack calculations, state updates, history processing, and claim handling.

The obsolete-repetition-history defect is a valid correction candidate, but it does not explain the engine's general weakness.

Use profiling and bounded prototypes to guide changes. Consider specialized internal search state where justified, but do not assume a wholesale rewrite is necessary.

Keep one authoritative implementation of legal moves, state transitions, history, and outcomes. Optimized search operations must preserve its behavior.

**Workstream B: search coverage and classical judgment**

Investigate why mShogi overlooks important defensive and tactical continuations or evaluates their consequences incorrectly.

Use the independent benchmark, complete game histories, matched-horizon comparisons, and controlled search configurations to distinguish:

- Insufficient useful search.
- Ineffective pruning, reductions, or move ordering.
- Problems at the search frontier.
- Incorrect classical evaluation of positions and compensation.

Select changes based on evidence. Do not add evaluation bonuses or special cases merely to repair individual historical positions.

Keep MSH-153 (Classical Compensation Calibration) on hold unless the broader investigation establishes that classical valuation deserves priority.

Coordinate both workstreams as one coherent engineering effort. They may proceed in parallel where responsibilities and file ownership permit.

## 5. Measure actual decision improvement

For each substantial engineering stage, evaluate the combined result against its frozen predecessor.

Record improvements, regressions, unchanged decisions, and unresolved judgments separately.

Use exact outcome tests where possible. For positional problems, inspect meaningful alternatives and their continuations rather than counting simple agreement with Stockfish.

Require improvement across a diverse collection, including genuinely held-out positions. Do not treat success on the motivating examples as sufficient evidence.

If a coherent round does not produce useful results, reconsider the underlying diagnosis instead of automatically generating another narrow correction task.

After substantial progress, propose a controlled playing-strength comparison against the predecessor and independent opponents. Do not execute a substantial tournament without my approval.

## 6. Engineering process and autonomy

Follow AGENTS.md and the repository's risk-based verification policy.

Use local verification for ordinary platform-independent engine work. Run cross-platform CI only when the change or integration checkpoint justifies it.

Use separate agents for independent review where beneficial, but avoid unnecessary coordination overhead.

Keep the roadmap forward-looking and the backlog readable. Use stable descriptive task handles and concise Problem, Objective, Approach, and Completion sections.

Handle routine research, implementation, testing, and engineering decisions autonomously. Do not request approval merely to move between related tasks in this authorized round.

Escalate major architectural changes, substantial resource commitments, and product decisions.

Do not begin learned evaluation or transfer the architecture to Shogi or Xiangqi.

## 7. Reporting

Keep conversational progress reports brief.

When the coherent round is complete, report:

1. The general weaknesses established by the investigation.
2. The main corrections and why they were selected.
3. The measured effect on search cost and decision quality at practical thinking times.
4. Results on independent held-out positions, including regressions.
5. Remaining significant limitations.
6. Whether the evidence justifies a new competitive strength comparison.

Preserve detailed technical evidence in the repository instead of reproducing it in conversational reports.

The central objective is to make mShogi choose substantially better Chess moves across many types of positions.

**We want to prove that the engine plays better Chess, not merely that it searches faster or passes more implementation tests.**
