# mShogi: Correct Conventional Chess Decision Failures

The previous engineering round, MSH-157 (Conventional Decision Improvement), did not achieve its main objective. It improved history and draw-claim processing but did not establish broadly better Chess decisions.

I want to correct our development direction.

**Your primary objective is to improve the Chess engine's decisions. Do not substitute unrelated performance optimizations, infrastructure improvements, documentation, or additional general assessments for this objective.**

Read the MSH-154 through MSH-157 reports, the current strength roadmap, and the relevant search code before starting. Use the existing benchmark and experimental tools.

## 1. Investigate the actual decision failures

Start with three established development positions:

- **0UOyM:** mShogi selects Kg6 instead of the reference-supported exf3+. Investigate the missed king route, blockade, and distant pawn race.
- **0m5mI:** mShogi selects Bd6 instead of Kc3 at five seconds. Investigate the king penetration and bishop-blockade consequences.
- **28pJK:** mShogi selects Qf4 instead of Qc8. Investigate why the important quiet defensive reply Qc1 does not receive adequate consideration.

These are starting points, not positions for special-case tuning. Reference analysis supports the alternative choices but does not prove their exact game outcomes.

Include successful controls. Look for a general cause that affects multiple positions.

## 2. Trace the real search

Previous investigations used separate child searches and broad pruning switches. These did not identify the cause.

This time, examine what happens inside the actual search.

Add focused, temporary diagnostic instrumentation where necessary. Trace relevant moves and their continuations through the existing search process.

For each important continuation, determine:

- Was the move generated and admitted?
- Was it searched, reduced, pruned, or excluded at the frontier?
- How much search depth remained?
- Which alpha-beta bounds, search windows, and transposition-table results affected it?
- Which positions were evaluated?
- How were scores returned to the parent?
- Where did the engine first fail to recognize or correctly value the important consequence?

Compare five-second and selected fifteen-second searches. Preserve their actual search conditions.

A new search from a child position is not equivalent to the original interior search. Do not use it as proof of what happened inside the original search.

Do not assume that failure to follow a Stockfish principal variation is itself a defect. Identify the first difference that matters to the quality of the decision.

## 3. Test general corrections

Once you have a plausible cause, test a general correction.

You are authorized to make experimental changes to conventional Chess search or classical evaluation.

You do not need proof that a proposed correction will improve playing strength before testing it. A sound technical hypothesis and a controlled experiment are sufficient.

Use established Chess-engine techniques where appropriate. Consider search reductions, pruning, extensions, frontier search, move ordering, and evaluation only when relevant to the observed failures.

Do not add position-specific rules or bonuses.

For each experiment:

1. State the suspected cause.
2. Make a focused change.
3. Compare decisions and search behavior against the frozen predecessor.
4. Check independent development positions and successful controls.
5. Keep a general correction if the evidence supports it. Otherwise revert it.

Do not spend the assignment on large parameter sweeps or cosmetic score improvements.

Do not require perfect causal proof before implementation, but require evidence before retaining a production change.

## 4. Strict scope

The following work is outside this assignment:

- Unrelated history or draw-claim optimization.
- General performance cleanup.
- New benchmark construction or expansion.
- Another broad architectural review.
- Learned evaluation.
- Shogi or Xiangqi development.
- A large competitive tournament.
- A wholesale engine rewrite without my approval.

If you discover an unrelated defect, record it briefly and continue the assigned work. Do not implement it unless it directly blocks the investigation.

Do not divide the assignment into a long sequence of new diagnostic tickets. Keep one clear engineering objective.

The goal is not to make the engine faster at performing the same ineffective search. The goal is to make it find and correctly assess important continuations.

## 5. Measure decision quality

Use the existing 240-position benchmark.

The 160 development positions may guide experiments. Keep the 80 held-out positions sealed until the candidate and evaluation procedure are frozen.

Use five seconds as the primary thinking-time budget. Retain one-second regression checks and selected fifteen-second diagnostic comparisons.

Measure meaningful decision outcomes, not only move agreement, reported scores, nominal depth, or node throughput.

Preserve exact tactical and tablebase outcomes. For other positions, use independent continuation analysis and distinguish supported improvements from uncertain judgments.

Once you have selected and frozen a candidate, evaluate it against the predecessor using the existing benchmark. Do not tune the candidate after seeing held-out results.

Reuse valid predecessor measurements where possible. Keep new active engine computation within 2,400 seconds unless I approve a larger budget. No opponent-match campaign is authorized.

A correction must show a supported benefit on the problem it addresses and acceptable behavior on wider development controls before you retain it. Claim broad improvement only when independent validation supports that conclusion.

## 6. If the investigation remains inconclusive

Do not abandon the decision-quality objective in favor of an unrelated task.

If the initial hypotheses fail, reconsider the search mechanism. Compare the relevant implementation with established conventional Chess search designs.

Investigate a different general explanation and test it.

However, do not continue indefinitely. After a reasonable set of distinct, focused experiments, stop if no correction has adequate support.

In that case, report the exact technical obstacle and the evidence needed to resolve it.

An inconclusive result is acceptable. Replacing the requested work with an unrelated optimization is not.

## 7. Engineering process

Follow AGENTS.md, the native rules authority requirement, and the risk-based verification policy.

Preserve exact legal moves, game histories, claims, outcomes, state restoration, and cancellation.

Use appropriate local correctness tests and independent review. Do not run unrelated platform or GUI test campaigns.

You may implement, review, and integrate supported conventional Chess corrections autonomously. Do not stop for approval between ordinary experiments.

Keep task descriptions and reports concise. Use Problem, Objective, Approach, and Completion. Store detailed traces in the repository.

MSH-153 (Classical Compensation Calibration) remains on hold unless this investigation establishes that a general valuation problem requires correction.

## 8. Completion

At the end, report:

1. What caused each investigated decision failure, or what remains unresolved.
2. What general correction you tested and why.
3. Which corrections you retained or rejected.
4. Whether the engine makes better decisions at five seconds.
5. What the development and held-out tests establish.
6. What significant problems remain.

If the work produces no supported decision improvement, state that clearly.

**Success means better Chess decisions, supported by independent evidence.**

Do not measure success by the number of optimizations, new tickets, tests, documents, or completed investigations.

Focus engineering effort on the search and evaluation mechanisms that determine move quality. Complete this assignment before returning to other development priorities.
